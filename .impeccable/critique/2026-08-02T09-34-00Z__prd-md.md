## Design Specificity Verdict

The material language is authored for Crays: coaster portraits, ticket-shaped feed items, a woven membership band, scanner-safe QR, and nightlife palette directly express the “objects of a night out” thesis. Structurally, the five concepts behave more like strong campaign frames than a complete service. They depict the live-room middle vividly but omit entry, commerce completion, durable order records, and the ongoing member relationship.

The deterministic scan returned `[]` because the repository contains Markdown and static PNGs rather than scannable UI code. This is not a clean bill of health. Visual inspection found concrete inconsistencies in every screen, including a visible table number that contradicts the PRD, color-only presence dots, missing active-room actions, and conflicting contact and order terminology.

## Overall Impression

Crays already has a memorable product world. Its biggest opportunity is to become the guest operating system for a venue relationship—not just a beautiful social room—by joining arrival, identity, ordering, access, and membership into one legible lifecycle.

## What’s Working

- The coaster, ticket, wristband, receipt, and menu metaphors create a specific and coherent visual world.
- Browse quietly, expiring presence, room locking, no exact distance, and venue-scoped access make privacy concrete.
- “The bar gets the order. Maya gets the ticket.” makes the social-to-operational handoff unusually clear.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The product has no entry architecture

Cold signup, invite redemption, and returning login are materially different intents but are not specified. Add an entry router that preserves venue/event/membership context through authentication and returns the person to the intended destination.

Suggested command: `$impeccable onboard`

### [P1] Active-room state and navigation contradict one another

The connected People screen still says “I’m here,” while promised actions such as Menu, My night, and Leave are absent. Separate pre-join and connected states, keep the active-room switch consistent, and define My night as contextual rather than archival.

Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Orders are requirements, not an end-to-end product journey

The PRD defines a status ladder and the screens show a gift selector plus one ready item, but omit modifiers, cart, checkout, confirmation, active tracking, receipt, history, cancellation, refund, and support. Define the whole journey and use one status vocabulary.

Suggested command: `$impeccable shape`

### [P1] Membership is represented as a badge, not a relationship

The current row communicates expiry only. Venue offer, benefits, status, usage ledger, presentation, renewal, payment action, terms, and failure states require a dedicated membership detail and durable home.

Suggested command: `$impeccable shape`

### [P1] Consent and transactional contracts diverge from the screens

Message appears beside Wizz despite Wizz being defined as the consent gate. The drink CTA looks charge-ready without a review step, payment method, decline rule, or refund path. Show Message only for eligible contacts and separate selection, review, payment, recipient response, and fulfillment.

Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan, first-timer:** No entry surface explains whether to create an identity, redeem an invite, log in, or join. “23 here now” plus “I’m here” makes presence publication feel ambiguous.

**Casey, distracted mobile user:** An interrupted invite or identity flow has no preservation contract. After payment or app switching, there is no demonstrated route back to the active order or door credential.

**Riley, stress tester:** Expired or already-redeemed invites, identity mismatch, payment success before app closure, gift decline after acceptance, membership grace periods, exhausted passes, offline QR, and revoked entitlement recovery are not joined into end-to-end flows.

## Minor Observations

- Screen 01 shows Jonas’s “TABLE 12,” directly contradicting the PRD’s privacy rule.
- “23 here now” should be “23 visible” if quiet guests are excluded.
- A Dating badge appears without a Dating filter and increases dating-app ambiguity.
- Wizz, Wave, and Say hi describe the same contact gesture inconsistently.
- The Bluetooth glyph foregrounds implementation detail despite the “protocols stay backstage” principle.
- “Show at the door” uses a contactless icon while presenting a QR.
- Membership uses a star affordance while pass and order rows use chevrons.

## Questions to Consider

- If nobody chooses to be visible, is Crays still compelling through ordering, entry, and membership alone?
- Is My night a contextual dashboard for now, or a permanent archive—and if contextual, why is Me selected?
- Does Dating intent create enough value to justify how strongly it pulls the product toward dating-app expectations?
- At what exact step has a drink been selected, authorized, ordered, accepted, and fulfilled?
